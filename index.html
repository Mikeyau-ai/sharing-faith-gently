The mindset

The Heart of Gentleness

People ask about our hope when our lives make them curious, at home and at work. Our character comes before our arguments.

Start with a cuppa and catch-up (15 min), then the blocks below.

Welcome

Open · Welcome

  1. Think of someone whose faith made you curious before they said a word about it. What did you notice?

Watch · 12 minutes

  • Book of 1 Peter Summary7:56BibleProjectPeter writes to Christians treated as outsiders and shows how their conduct can bear witness to Jesus.
  • Hope4:34BibleProjectBiblical hope rests on a person, not on circumstances. It is the "hope that you have" in 1 Peter 3:15.

After the videos · Videos

  1. Peter's readers were treated as outsiders. Where do you feel like an outsider for your faith, at work or at home?
  2. Biblical hope rests on a person, not circumstances. How would your family or workmates know you have that kind of hope?

Scripture

1 Peter 3:13–17
Readiness, gentleness, respect and a clear conscience, even when mistreated.
Colossians 4:5–6
Wisdom toward outsiders, and speech "seasoned with salt".
Titus 2:9–10
Trustworthy work can make the teaching about God attractive.

Context: Titus 2:9–10 is written to slaves in a Roman household. Apply it carefully to employees. Trustworthy work can make the gospel attractive. It is not a licence to put up with abuse or injustice.

Word study: the Greek word for gentleness here, prautēs, is often described as strength under control, not weakness.

Into the text · Scripture

  1. In 1 Peter 3:15, what comes first: revering Christ, or giving an answer? Why might the order matter?
  2. Titus 2:9–10 says the way we work can make the gospel attractive. What would that look like in your job, and what would make it unattractive?

Discussion

Bring it home · Discussion

  1. Have you ever won an argument at home but pushed your kids away? What would gentleness have changed?
  2. What makes you defensive when your kids question your faith, or a colleague does? What would strength under control look like then?

Practice · gentle answers

In pairs, one person plays a workmate or one of your kids and raises a common criticism, such as "Christians are so judgemental" or "Church is boring and pointless." The other answers in under a minute, aiming for gentleness and respect rather than winning. Swap, then talk about what tone came through.

Prayer

Thank God for someone whose gentle life drew you towards Jesus.

Ask for a gentle spirit the next time faith comes up at home or at work.

The strategy

The Art of Asking Questions

Jesus often answered with a question. Listening first shows people they matter and earns us the right to be heard.

Start with a cuppa and catch-up (15 min), then the blocks below.

Welcome

Open · Welcome

  1. Who in your life is a great listener? What do they do that makes you feel heard?

Watch · 12 minutes

  • Everyday Evangelism: How to Start7:43Sam ChanAsk a safe question like "Do you have a faith?", then listen to their story for as long as they will tell it.
  • How to Defend Your Faith Like a Pro—Without Arguing!4:07Greg Koukl, Stand to ReasonThe "Columbo tactic": well-chosen questions instead of preaching, even at work.

After the videos · Videos

  1. Sam Chan suggests asking "Do you have a faith?" and then listening for as long as they will talk. What would make that question feel safe rather than awkward?
  2. Koukl suggests using questions where you can't openly talk about faith, like many workplaces. What would a genuine question sound like there, rather than a trap?

Scripture

Luke 10:25–37
Jesus answers a test with questions and ends by asking who was the neighbour.
Mark 10:46–52
"What do you want me to do for you?"
James 1:19
Quick to listen, slow to speak, slow to become angry.

Into the text · Scripture

  1. In Luke 10 the expert asks to test Jesus. How do Jesus's questions turn a debate into a personal challenge?
  2. Why would Jesus ask a blind man what he wants (Mark 10:51)? What does a question give the other person?

Discussion

Bring it home · Discussion

  1. What makes it hard to listen to your kids, or to a colleague, without jumping in to correct them?
  2. What would change if your aim in a conversation was to understand, rather than to persuade?

Practice · the question ladder

In pairs, one person plays a workmate, or one of your kids home from uni, whose view differs. For three minutes the other may only ask questions: no opinions and no corrections. Climb the ladder: "What do you mean by that?", "How did you come to that view?", "What has that been like for you?" Swap, then talk about how it felt to be listened to.

Prayer

Ask God for real curiosity about your family and the people you work with.

Pray that you would listen longer than you speak in one conversation this week.

Before Day 3

Questions you might ask during the week:

  • Did you grow up with any faith or spirituality?
  • What gives you hope when life gets hard?
  • What makes work worth doing, beyond the pay?
  • What do you most want your life to be about?
  • What's your impression of Christians, and where did it come from?
  • If God were real, what would you want to ask him?

Go deeper

Optional, between sessions.

The twelve-year-old Jesus listening to the teachers and asking questions.

Answering before listening is folly.

The message

Telling a Better Story

People can argue with our theology, but not with what God has done in our lives. Tell it simply, and let it point to Jesus.

Start with a cuppa and catch-up (15 min), then the blocks below.

Welcome

Open · Welcome

  1. What's a story you love to tell, such as a trip, a rescue or a near miss? What makes it fun to tell?

Watch · 17 minutes

  • Everyday Evangelism: How to Share Your Story10:33Sam ChanDrop the jargon and shape your story in three parts: who you are and what you long for, the moment it turns out to be all about Jesus, and life with him now.
  • Why Is My Evangelism at Work So Lame?5:58John Piper, Desiring GodInvite a colleague to lunch and offer to trade life stories, and don't push if they decline.

After the videos · Videos

  1. Sam Chan says "I grew up in a Christian family and decided for myself at 16" means little to most outsiders. Why? What would help that same story land?

Scripture

John 9:24–34
Under questioning he admits what he doesn't know, and says the one thing he does.
Deuteronomy 6:20–25
When a son asks why, the father answers with the family's story.
Mark 5:18–20
"Go home to your own people and tell them."

Into the text · Scripture

  1. In John 9 the man admits what he doesn't know (verse 25). Why is that honesty so persuasive?
  2. In Deuteronomy 6:20–21 the child asks about the rules, and his father answers with a story. Why a story rather than a lecture?

Discussion

Bring it home · Discussion

  1. What church words do you use without noticing ("saved", "fellowship", "quiet time")? How would you say each in plain words?
  2. What difference does Jesus make in your life this week? Would your kids or your workmates say they've seen it?

Practice · plain words, then your story

Put the hope of the gospel into two plain sentences that answer "What do Christians actually believe?" One sentence for what Jesus has done, one for what it means for us. Everyday words only. If you stall: Jesus died and rose to bring us back to God, and life with him starts now and doesn't end.

Jot the sentences and the three boxes, briefly. With a partner, take two minutes each: the sentences, then your story, as if to a workmate or to one of your kids. One encouragement, flag any jargon, then swap.

Prayer

Thank God for something specific he has done in your life.

Ask for plain words when you tell that story, and for a natural opening with your family or a workmate.

The outcome

Living with Open Hands

Our part is to plant and water faithfully, and God gives the growth. That frees us to love our kids and our colleagues with patience, not pressure.

Start with a cuppa and catch-up (15 min), then the blocks below.

Welcome

Open · Welcome

  1. Who was patient with you while you were working out faith or life? What did they do?

Watch · 9 minutes

  • What Can a Parent Do When Their Grown Children Have Walked Away from Christianity?4:22Stand to ReasonPray, ask questions and listen patiently. Each conversation doesn't need the whole gospel, and it may take years.
  • God Gives the Growth: 1 Corinthians 3:6–8 Explained4:07Jim Murrell, The Passage UnlockedFocus on your part, not the outcome: God rewards faithfulness, not results.

After the videos · Videos

  1. The first video says each conversation doesn't need to cover the whole gospel. How would that change the way you talk with your kids?
  2. The second video says God rewards faithfulness, not results. Does that relieve you or unsettle you? Why?

Scripture

1 Corinthians 3:5–9
Paul planted, Apollos watered, God gave the growth.
Luke 15:11–24
The father lets his son go, keeps watching, and runs to meet him.
Mark 4:26–29
The seed grows, "though he does not know how".

Into the text · Scripture

  1. In 1 Corinthians 3:5, what does Paul call himself and Apollos? What does that say about how we should see ourselves?
  2. In Luke 15:20, what is the father doing while his son is away? What does he do when the son returns, and what does he not do?

Discussion

Bring it home · Discussion

  1. Where do you feel pressure to "close the deal", with your kids or at work? Where does that pressure come from?
  2. If your kids, or someone else you love, never respond to Jesus, how will you keep loving them with no strings attached?

Practice · one person, one act

Write down one person: one of your kids, someone else in your family, or a colleague. Plan one practical way to serve them or spend time with them in the next two weeks, such as a meal, a job done together, or help with a problem at work, with no expectation attached. In threes, name the person and the plan. Keep it short; you will pray next.

Prayer

Gentleness, good questions, a plain story, and open hands: that is this course. Keep going with one person, and leave the growth with God.

Thank God that the growth is his work.

Pray by name for the person you chose, and ask to be patient and generous with them.
